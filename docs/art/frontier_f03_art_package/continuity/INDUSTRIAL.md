# Industrial District Continuity

## Role

The Industrial District is Frontier's center for processing, workshops, heavy storage, machinery, refining, fabrication, and later specialist crafting.

## Canonical city position

- Southeast / eastern working edge of the main city.
- East of or adjacent to the broader waterfront economy.
- Market District lies inland / northwest.
- Transit / Outskirts access should be nearby or visually plausible.
- Water, canal, river, or utility edge may remain visible depending on framing.

## Required visual anchors

Every approved Industrial image should preserve the identity of these anchors:

1. **Refinery / Processing Complex**
   - visually prominent;
   - pipes, vents, furnaces, tanks, chimneys, or processing structures;
   - should be recognizable even from the city overview.

2. **Blacksmith / Heavy Workshop**
   - smaller and more craft-oriented than the refinery;
   - forge, chimney, loading doors, metal stock, or workshop yard.

3. **Gunsmith / Precision Workshop**
   - compact specialist workshop;
   - cleaner/more controlled than heavy industrial buildings;
   - can later support broader machinery/precision-crafting themes if game design evolves.

4. **Warehouses**
   - storage-oriented buildings;
   - connected to loading areas and roads.

5. **Loading / Utility Yard**
   - carts, pallets/crates, machinery, utility sheds, or service lanes;
   - should visually connect Industrial to logistics systems.

## Canonical sublocations

- Refinery
- Blacksmith
- Gunsmith / Precision Workshop
- Warehouses
- Loading Yard

## Internal movement logic

The district should imply:

- raw materials arrive through warehouse/loading areas;
- refinery is connected to bulk storage;
- workshops sit closer to roads and player-accessible streets;
- specialist workshops are accessible without requiring entry into heavy refinery grounds;
- an outward road can plausibly connect toward Transit / Outskirts.

## Material language

Primary materials:

- brick;
- stone;
- dark timber;
- iron/steel;
- corrugated or riveted metal;
- soot-stained masonry;
- practical glazing;
- pipes and vents.

Palette:

- muted brick red;
- warm stone;
- charcoal;
- iron gray;
- dark green/olive industrial accents;
- restrained cream signage.

## Atmosphere

The Industrial District should feel:

- productive;
- dense;
- practical;
- slightly noisier/heavier than other districts;
- important to the city's economy.

It should **not** feel:

- apocalyptic;
- toxic by default;
- steampunk-fantasy;
- modern corporate industrial park;
- sci-fi megafactory.

## Clickability and composition

Artwork should leave readable zones for:

- Refinery
- Blacksmith
- Precision/Gunsmith workshop
- Warehouse area
- Loading Yard

Use distinct building silhouettes and materials to make the locations understandable before labels appear.

## Flexible elements

These may vary:

- carts/vehicles;
- stack of materials;
- workers;
- smoke amount;
- crates;
- temporary scaffolding;
- small utility sheds;
- parked machinery;
- barrels/pallets.

## Must not drift

- Refinery remains the major industrial landmark.
- Warehouse/loading relationship stays logical.
- Workshops remain more player-accessible than the refinery core.
- District remains east/southeast relative to Market and Harbor.
- Architectural/material identity remains heavier than Market/Civic.
- Major road connection toward the city and future transit remains recognizable.

## Day/night variants

Night Industrial should derive from the daytime composition.

Potential overlays:

- furnace/window glow;
- sparse yard lights;
- chimney smoke;
- limited work lamps;
- subtle reflections on metal/wet surfaces.

Do not alter geography or hotspot positions.

## Future animation-friendly layers

Possible isolated overlays:

- chimney smoke;
- vent steam;
- furnace glow flicker;
- slow crane/hoist movement;
- rotating fan;
- occasional cart/vehicle motion;
- workshop spark effect.

Animations should remain subtle enough that the UI stays readable.
